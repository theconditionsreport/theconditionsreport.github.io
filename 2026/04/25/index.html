The Forecast

The Southern Hemisphere Just Woke Up

Saturday morning and while most of the Northern Hemisphere is doing the math on how many corn laps are left, New Zealand just opened a ski area — in April — because the Southern Hemisphere has decided winter starts now. Hanmer Springs is spinning lifts after an early-season dump, and after last year's disappointing Kiwi season, the energy down under is cautiously electric. Back up here, Palisades Tahoe just extended into May after catching 11 inches of April snow, which means California still has a resort running and the season refuses to flatline. The Wasatch has 18 centimeters in the three-day forecast, so if you wrote off Utah, you wrote off too early. Mammoth is Mammoth. Killington's Superstar is doing what Superstar does. And today in Whistler, the community gathers on snow to celebrate the life of Kai Smart — a reminder that the mountains hold everything, the joy and the grief, and the people who show up for both are the ones who matter.

Off-Piste

🔹 A 100-foot ice block is holding up Everest's spring climbing season. The Khumbu Icefall — the same terrain Andrzej Bargiel skied last fall — has a massive chunk of ice blocking the standard route, delaying rope-fixing operations and potentially pushing summit windows later than usual. SnowBrains

🔹 Brodie Mountain in Massachusetts might actually come back from the dead. The abandoned ski area in the Berkshires, shuttered since 2002, has new ownership and plans are emerging for a revival. The East Coast lost-ski-area community just got its first real piece of good news in years. Unofficial Networks

🔹 Breckenridge and Brian Head are adding bonus days after officially ending their seasons. When late-season storms arrive after you've already called it, apparently the move is to just... un-call it. Breck adds surprise laps for passholders, and Brian Head follows suit. The Inertia

🔹 RIP Bernie Rosow. The Mammoth Mountain community lost a beloved groomer, skier, and all-around mountain person. Known for his park skills, deep-snow instincts, and the kind of local knowledge that makes a mountain home. He'll be missed. Powder Magazine

Europe mixed

The Alps are holding thin coverage at glacier altitude with temps from 0–8°C and no new snow expected; Scandinavia is the bright spot with 6cm in 24 hours, 11cm for the week, and 13cm in the three-day forecast under cold temps and midnight sun. →
