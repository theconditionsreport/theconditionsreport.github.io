The Forecast

Last Call, First Turns

It's the penultimate day at most major resorts, and instead of eulogies, we're getting plot twists. Natural Selection Ski goes live from Alaska tonight at 7 p.m. ET — the biggest backcountry freestyle event of the season, broadcasting from terrain that makes your home mountain look like a bunny hill. Meanwhile, Black Mountain in New Hampshire just declared it's skiing to Memorial Day, which is the kind of small-mountain audacity that keeps this sport honest. The PNW pulled 36 centimeters in the last 24 hours and has more coming. Jackson's looking at 23 centimeters by midweek. Even Jay Peak is out there carving spring corn through the trees like it's nobody's business. Tomorrow the gates close at a lot of places. Today, though? Today there's still skiing to be done, and the mountains aren't finished talking.

Conditions Check

Pacific Northwest mixed

A 36cm dump in the last 24 hours with another 7cm in the three-day forecast and temps running -8°C to -1°C — that's real snow on minimal base, so open terrain will be limited but what's there is fresh. →

Rockies closed

Colorado is essentially zeroed out — no base depth, no meaningful snowfall in the forecast, and Utah's Cottonwoods are reading 0cm on the ground with only a dusting expected midweek. →

Northern Rockies / Interior BC mixed

Jackson is sitting on minimal base but has 23cm in the three-day forecast with temps from -7°C to 1°C; Revelstoke picked up 14cm in 24 hours on a near-zero base — storm snow is coming but coverage remains thin. →

Northeast spring

Jay Peak is skiing spring corn through the trees and Killington's Superstar will grind into May — temps are warm (4°C to 19°C) and there's no new snow coming, but on-the-ground reports say conditions are genuinely good for the diehards. →

Europe thin

The Alps are down to 1-2cm base across France, Switzerland, and Austria with zero snowfall in the forecast; glacier operations and high-altitude terrain are the only game left, with cold temps (-5°C to -1°C) at least keeping what's there from vanishing. →

Japan closed

Zero snow depth across both Hokkaido and Honshu with temps running 4°C to 17°C and nothing in the forecast — the book is closed until next December. →
