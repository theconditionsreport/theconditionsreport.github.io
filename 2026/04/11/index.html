The Forecast

The Sierra Won't Quit

Forget the countdown for a minute. While the rest of the ski world is measuring its final days in costume ideas and pond-skim logistics, the Sierra is out here doing something unreasonable — 9 inches in the last 24 hours and another 21 inches in the forecast through Tuesday. Palisades and Mammoth are getting a legitimate April reload, temps in the low 20s, the kind of storm that makes you reconsider every plan you made for next week. Meanwhile, the Northern Rockies are quietly loading up a midweek surprise of their own — 10 to 20 inches possible Wednesday through Friday. The rest of the map? It's what you'd expect four days out from the big shutdown: thin, warm, and winding down. But spring has always been about knowing where to look, and right now the look is due west, into the teeth of a Pacific storm that didn't read the calendar.

Northeast thin

No measurable base at Jay Peak, Killington, or Mount Snow with temps ranging 25–43°F and 4cm in the past week — Killington may still be running limited terrain on machine-made snow, but natural coverage is gone. →

Europe thin

Bases of 1-2cm across the Alps with warm temps in the mid-30s to 46°F, but a pulse of up to 30cm is expected Sunday night through Tuesday along the Swiss-Italian border — glacier terrain could see a brief refresh. →

Japan closed

Zero snow on the ground across Hokkaido and Honshu with temps climbing into the 40s–50s°F and no snowfall in the forecast — the book is closed. →
