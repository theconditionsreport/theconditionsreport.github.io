The Forecast

Schirmer skis the gnarliest line of his career

Nikolai Schirmer just dropped a film about skiing what he calls the gnarliest line of his career — the Midnight Lightning on Anderstinden in Norway's Lyngen Alps — alongside childhood hero Jacob Wester, and the footage is the kind of thing that makes you set your coffee down and forget to breathe. Meanwhile, the World Cup opens in Sölden on Sunday with Team GB's Freddy Carrick-Smith racing giant slalom, which means the 26/27 competitive season officially has a start date. The Snow League announced Season Two tickets are live for Aspen in January — halfpipe's best on Buttermilk, finals starting at $25. Down in the Andes, Valle Nevado just ticked to 719 centimeters of base. That's 23.5 feet, and the lifts are still spinning. Up to 35cm is forecast for the western Alps this weekend, Whistler's peaks are getting dusted, and October continues to deliver receipts. Friday has altitude.

The Edit

Nikolai Schirmer links up with childhood hero Jacob Wester to attempt the Midnight Lightning line on Anderstinden in Norway's Lyngen Alps — and Schirmer calls it the gnarliest line of his career. That's not hyperbole from someone who's spent years documenting his own progression in increasingly consequential terrain. The film captures the decision-making, the exposure, and the trust required when two generations of Norwegian freeskiing meet on a face that demands everything. Black Crows backs the project, and the Lyngen footage alone is worth your full attention.
