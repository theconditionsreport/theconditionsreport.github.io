The Forecast

Chile's dream storm turned nightmare

The same storm that buried Chile's ski resorts under nearly fifteen feet of snow in a single week has now triggered catastrophic flooding and mudslides across central Chile, prompting a presidential state of emergency. It's the duality of mountain weather distilled into one brutal headline: Valle Nevado sitting on a dream base while communities downhill are getting wrecked. Argentina's stacking too — Las Leñas and Cerro Catedral picked up close to a meter in the past week with another 23cm forecast through the weekend. New Zealand's coverage remains scrappy but cold temps are holding what's there. Meanwhile, the Northern Hemisphere is doing Northern Hemisphere things: Mt Bachelor just opened its flagship downhill MTB trail, Mammoth is already teasing opening day for next winter, and a bison in Yellowstone reminded everyone that wildlife doesn't care about your rental car's tire warranty. The Andes are the story right now — for better and worse.

Conditions Check

Chile firing

177cm in the past seven days with another 21cm forecast — this is a historic storm cycle, though flooding has impacted access roads; check conditions before driving.

Argentina good

98cm in the past week across Las Leñas and Cerro Catedral with 23cm more in the forecast and temps holding between -5°C and 1°C — the Andes are stacking nicely.

New Zealand thin

9cm in the last 24 hours and cold temps (-6°C to -2°C) are preserving what's there, but coverage remains limited with only 13cm in the past week across Treble Cone and Mt Hutt.

Australia thin

Zero new snow and no precipitation in the forecast — Perisher, Thredbo, and Falls Creek are waiting for a proper storm cycle with temps hovering around freezing.

Oregon / Summer closed

No snow on the Palmer Snowfield with temps hitting 16°C — Timberline's summer skiing appears done for now.

Alps — Summer closed

Zermatt, Hintertux, and Tignes showing no snow and temps up to 11°C — glacier skiing is on pause across the board.

 

Same storm, two realities — fifteen feet of blower on the peaks, floodwater in the valleys. Respect the mountains. They're not here for us.
