The Forecast

Triple front flip at Whistler

Jérémie Paquette just threw a triple front flip off a kicker at Whistler — three full rotations, casual as a morning stretch — and Bruno Beaudoin caught the whole thing on camera for TGR. That's the energy heading into week two of September. Meanwhile, the Andes are still absurdly deep: Valle Nevado holding at 533 centimeters, Portillo at 272cm, La Parva at 236cm, and Las Leñas across the border at 234cm. The season down south has been historic and it's not done yet. But it wasn't all good news in Chile this weekend — a fire tore through the Hotel Alto Nevados at Nevados de Chillán, leveling the hotel and nearby buildings. Everyone got out safely, and the resort is set to reopen today. Stateside, Timberline just dropped season pass prices for 2026-27, the European Space Agency is adding its voice to the growing Super El Niño chorus, and POWDER dug up the story behind one of their most iconic covers ever. Tuesday's got layers.

Off-Piste

🔥 A fire destroyed the Hotel Alto Nevados and several nearby buildings at Nevados de Chillán, Chile, on Sunday. All guests and employees evacuated safely — a miracle given how fast it spread. The resort announced a phased reopening starting today, September 8th. The cause is still under investigation. SnowBrains | Unofficial Networks

🎿 Timberline Lodge, Oregon, just slashed season pass prices ahead of 2026-27 and added new perks for passholders. The Palmer Snowfield is still spinning lifts for summer skiing, and lower prices could make Timberline one of the better value plays in the Pacific Northwest this year. SnowBrains

🏔️ Sasquatch Mountain Resort in British Columbia is trying to rebuild trust after a brutal 2025-26 season that saw a key chairlift (Green Chair) never operate at all and a reduced schedule that left passholders fuming. The resort is promising improvements, but the goodwill account is deeply overdrawn. Unofficial Networks

🌍 The USDA's environmental impact statement on repealing the Roadless Rule is drawing fire from the outdoor community. Powder Magazine breaks down what the repeal would actually mean for ski access and backcountry terrain — and the short version is: nothing good. Powder Magazine
