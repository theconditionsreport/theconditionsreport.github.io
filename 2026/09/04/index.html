The Forecast

Triple hurricanes and the El Niño machine

Three hurricanes are spinning across the Pacific simultaneously — Karina, Lowell, and Marie — the first triple-header since 1974, and it's all being fueled by the same El Niño pattern that every forecaster in the Northern Hemisphere is watching with wide eyes. The Andes, meanwhile, remain absurdly deep: Valle Nevado is holding at 533 centimeters, Portillo at 295cm, La Parva at 244cm, Las Leñas at 241cm. That's an entire hemisphere of skiing that refuses to quit. TGR's office is apparently losing it with 15 days until the world premiere of Actually, It Is Rocket Science in Jackson Hole — their latest teaser has the whole crew making crash noises, which feels about right. Backcountry Magazine just sounded the alarm on the USDA's proposal to gut the Roadless Rule, New Zealand's Mount Cheeseman is already done for the season, and meteorologist Matt Makens just dropped his own El Niño winter forecast. Friday has teeth.

Off-Piste

🎿 Ski Santa Fe is pushing a major snowmaking upgrade proposal after the resort recorded just 70 inches of natural snowfall last season — roughly half its annual average. The plan would add coverage to key terrain and buy insurance against the kind of seasons that are becoming less exceptional. Powder

🏔️ The FIS Alpine Junior World Championships are headed back to the U.S. for the first time in 33 years. Whiteface Mountain in Lake Placid, NY, will host from March 2-12, 2027 — a legitimate statement that East Coast racing infrastructure can still play on the world stage. SnowBrains

🌊 Three simultaneous Pacific hurricanes — Karina, Lowell, and Marie — are churning between Mexico and Hawaii, a phenomenon not seen since 1974. El Niño is supercharging the Pacific basin and meteorologists are paying close attention to what this means for the incoming North American winter. Surfer

🎬 TGR's internal countdown to the September 19th premiere of Actually, It Is Rocket Science has apparently devolved into the whole staff making crash-sound effects on camera. Fifteen days out. TGR
